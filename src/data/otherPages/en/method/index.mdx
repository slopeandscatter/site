Every piece on this site follows the same shape: **a question, a model, something you
can drive, and the full working underneath.** This page is what that means in practice.

## The question

A question earns a piece when it is the kind of thing you would argue about at a bar,
and tight enough that a number can settle it.

- It has to be answerable. "Is transit underfunded?" is not a question, it is a mood.
  "What does one metre of this tunnel cost, and what else costs that?" is a question.
- It has to be unmeasured. If someone has already done the arithmetic properly, we link
  to them instead of redoing it worse.
- We do not run a piece we already know the answer to. If the model cannot surprise us,
  it cannot surprise you either.

## The model

Every answer is a model, and every model is a pile of assumptions. We would rather show
you the pile.

- Real unit costs, real rates, real quantities, each one sourced.
- We use the cheapest source that is good enough, and say which one. A national
  statistics agency where one exists; a vendor price list where it does not; our own
  measurement where neither does.
- Where the data runs out, we say so and estimate — and the estimate is labelled as one
  everywhere it appears, not just in a footnote.
- Assumptions are listed in one place, not scattered through the prose. If you disagree
  with one, you should be able to find it in a few seconds.

## Your turn

The model ships as a thing you can drive, not a picture of a thing we drove.

- The inputs we varied are the inputs you can vary. Change the route, the wage, the
  scope; the answer moves with you.
- Our headline number is just one setting of the controls. It is not privileged, and the
  piece says which setting it is.

## The receipts

At the bottom of every piece, in full:

- every source, linked, with the date it was accessed;
- every fudge factor and what it is standing in for;
- every place the model could be wrong, written by us before anyone else has to.

## How we state uncertainty

A confident-looking chart is a claim. We try not to make claims the data will not carry.

- Ranges, not false precision. A number quoted to four significant figures is usually a
  lie about how well it is known.
- We would rather publish a wide range than a precise wrong number.
- This is enforced in the furniture, not just the prose. The scatter on the front page
  will not draw a trend line through the archive unless the slope is actually
  distinguishable from zero at 95% — when it is not, it says so and draws nothing.

## Corrections

We will get things wrong. The interesting question is what happens next.

- A correction is a change to the piece **and** a line in the next email. Fixing it
  quietly is not fixing it.
- The original number stays visible, struck through, with the date it changed. Pieces
  are not silently rewritten.
- Errors in the model are treated the same as errors in the prose. A wrong assumption is
  a correction, not a revision.

## The desks

A piece belongs to exactly one desk, chosen by what the question is *about* rather than
who it affects. Each desk has a colour, and that colour is the same everywhere the desk
appears — in the navigation, on a tag, and as a point in the scatter on the front page.

| Desk | What lives there |
| --- | --- |
| Infrastructure | What things cost to build, and what they cost to not build |
| Cost of living | Rent, groceries, bills, wages — the arithmetic of getting by |
| Games | Games as systems with numbers in them |
| Sport | Performance, money, and the gap between them |
| Culture | Film, music, books, and the economics underneath |

## What we don't do

- We do not take money from anyone we write about.
- We do not publish a number we cannot source or reproduce.
- We do not use a chart to say something the underlying data does not.
